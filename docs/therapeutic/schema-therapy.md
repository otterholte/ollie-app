# Schema Therapy for Ollie

## Overview

Schema Therapy addresses deep, enduring patterns (schemas) formed in childhood that continue to cause problems in adult life. It's particularly effective for self-loathing, chronic depression, BPD, and "feeling broken."

**Important**: Schema work is for **Reflection Mode only** — never during acute distress.

---

## Core Concepts

### What Are Schemas?

Early Maladaptive Schemas are:
- Deep beliefs about yourself, others, and the world
- Formed in childhood through unmet needs
- Feel absolutely true (even when irrational)
- Self-perpetuating (we recreate what's familiar)
- Trigger strong emotions when activated

**Example**:
> "I'm fundamentally flawed" (Defectiveness Schema)
> Feels like fact, not belief

---

## The 18 Early Maladaptive Schemas

### Domain 1: Disconnection & Rejection
*Core: "I won't be loved/accepted"*

#### 1. Abandonment
- Fear of being left
- Belief that relationships are unstable
- "Everyone leaves eventually"

#### 2. Mistrust/Abuse
- Expecting harm from others
- "People will hurt me if I let them"
- Hypervigilance to betrayal

#### 3. Emotional Deprivation
- Belief that emotional needs won't be met
- Feeling chronically unseen
- "No one really understands me"

#### 4. Defectiveness/Shame
- Feeling fundamentally flawed
- Deep shame about who you are
- "If people really knew me, they'd reject me"

#### 5. Social Isolation
- Feeling different from others
- Not belonging anywhere
- "I don't fit in"

### Domain 2: Impaired Autonomy
*Core: "I can't function/survive"*

#### 6. Dependence/Incompetence
- Belief that you can't handle life
- Needing others to function
- "I can't do this alone"

#### 7. Vulnerability to Harm
- Expecting catastrophe
- Feeling unable to cope with bad events
- "Something terrible will happen"

#### 8. Enmeshment
- Over-involvement with others
- No separate identity
- "I don't know who I am without them"

#### 9. Failure
- Belief that you'll inevitably fail
- Feeling inadequate compared to peers
- "I'm not as capable as others"

### Domain 3: Impaired Limits
*Core: "Rules don't apply to me" or "I can't control myself"*

#### 10. Entitlement
- Believing you deserve special treatment
- Difficulty with reciprocity
- "I shouldn't have to follow normal rules"

#### 11. Insufficient Self-Control
- Difficulty with frustration tolerance
- Impulsivity, addiction-prone
- "I can't stop myself"

### Domain 4: Other-Directedness
*Core: "Others' needs matter more"*

#### 12. Subjugation
- Suppressing own needs for others
- Fear of anger/abandonment if assertive
- "What I want doesn't matter"

#### 13. Self-Sacrifice
- Excessive focus on others' needs
- Neglecting self
- "I have to take care of everyone"

#### 14. Approval-Seeking
- Excessive need for validation
- Self-worth depends on others' opinions
- "I need people to like me"

### Domain 5: Overvigilance & Inhibition
*Core: "I must prevent bad things"*

#### 15. Negativity/Pessimism
- Focus on negative aspects
- Expecting the worst
- "Things will go wrong"

#### 16. Emotional Inhibition
- Suppressing emotions
- Appearing cold or controlled
- "Showing feelings is dangerous"

#### 17. Unrelenting Standards
- Perfectionism
- Never good enough
- "I must achieve more"

#### 18. Punitiveness
- Harsh self-criticism
- Believing mistakes deserve punishment
- "I should suffer for my failures"

## Schema Modes

Modes are the moment-to-moment emotional states triggered by schemas.

### Child Modes
**Vulnerable Child**: Fear, sadness, loneliness, shame
**Angry Child**: Rage at unmet needs
**Impulsive Child**: Acting on urges without thinking
**Happy Child**: Feeling loved, content, playful

### Maladaptive Coping Modes
**Compliant Surrenderer**: Giving in to schemas
**Detached Protector**: Numbing, disconnecting
**Overcompensator**: Acting opposite to schema (e.g., controlling when feel powerless)

### Dysfunctional Parent Modes
**Punitive Parent**: Inner critic, harsh judgment
**Demanding Parent**: Impossible standards, never satisfied

### The Goal
**Healthy Adult**: Balanced, nurturing to self, meets needs appropriately

## Tools for Ollie

### Schema Identification

**Purpose**: Help users recognize their patterns

**App Implementation**:
- Questionnaire-style exploration (not diagnostic)
- "Does this resonate?" approach
- Show 2-3 most relevant schemas
- Explain without pathologizing

**Questions to explore**:
- "When you feel worst about yourself, what's the belief underneath?"
- "What did you learn to believe about yourself growing up?"
- "What triggers your strongest reactions?"

**Ollie's approach**:
> "These patterns often form early. Recognizing them doesn't mean they're true — it means we can start to see when they're running the show."

### Schema Trigger Tracking

**Purpose**: Notice when schemas activate

**App Implementation**:
- Log moments of intense reaction
- Connect to potential schema
- Track patterns over time
- "What just got triggered?"

**Pattern insights**:
> "Defectiveness seems to activate most after social situations."

### Mode Awareness

**Purpose**: Identify current emotional state

**App Implementation**:
- "What mode are you in right now?"
- Simple options:
  - Vulnerable (scared, sad, ashamed)
  - Critical (harsh on yourself)
  - Protective (numb, shut down)
  - Healthy (balanced, present)
- Track mode patterns

### Punitive Parent vs Healthy Adult

**Purpose**: Externalize and respond to inner critic

**App Implementation**:
1. Write what the inner critic says
2. Identify it as "Punitive Parent" mode
3. Write what a Healthy Adult would say
4. Notice the difference

**Example**:
- **Critic**: "You're so stupid. You always mess up."
- **Healthy Adult**: "You made a mistake. Everyone does. What can you learn?"

### Limited Reparenting (Self-Compassion Focus)

**Purpose**: Give yourself what you needed

**Concept**: You can't change the past, but you can provide for your needs now.

**App Implementation**:
- Identify unmet need (validation, safety, acceptance)
- Practice self-compassion statements
- Visualize nurturing your younger self
- Build Healthy Adult capacity

**Note**: This is gentle, not dramatic. Not about reliving trauma.

## Integration with Ollie's Model

### NOT for Right Now Mode
Schema work requires stability. Never explore schemas during crisis.

### Daily Mode
- Light awareness: "Notice if a strong reaction comes up today"
- Track potential triggers (optional)

### Reflection Mode (Primary Use)
- Schema exploration
- Pattern identification
- Mode tracking over time
- Healthy Adult building

---

## Safety Considerations

### When NOT to Explore Schemas
- During acute distress
- If user is dissociating
- Without grounding skills in place
- If it's retraumatizing
- If user isn't ready

### Ollie's Safety Approach
> "This work can bring up a lot. If it feels like too much, we can pause anytime. There's no rush."

### Always Offer Exit
- "Take a break" button
- Return to Daily Mode
- Crisis resources visible

---

## Ollie's Schema Therapy Voice

**Validating the struggle**:
> "These patterns formed for a reason. They were probably protective once. But you're not stuck with them."

**Naming without pathologizing**:
> "This sounds like it might be connected to feeling 'not good enough' — something many people carry from childhood."

**Building hope**:
> "Recognizing a pattern is the first step. It means you can start to choose differently, even if it's hard."

**Normalizing difficulty**:
> "This is deep stuff. It makes sense that it's uncomfortable. We can go as slow as you need."

## Sample Ollie Prompts

**Introducing schemas**:
> "Sometimes we carry beliefs about ourselves from way back. They feel like facts but they're more like... old stories. Want to explore what yours might be?"

**Identifying a schema**:
> "That feeling of being fundamentally flawed — it's called the Defectiveness schema. A lot of people have it. It doesn't mean it's true."

**Tracking patterns**:
> "I notice this feeling comes up a lot after you compare yourself to others. Does that pattern feel familiar?"

**Building Healthy Adult**:
> "What would you say to a friend who felt this way? Can you try saying that to yourself?"

**Normalizing the work**:
> "This isn't about fixing something wrong with you. It's about understanding patterns so they don't run your life."
