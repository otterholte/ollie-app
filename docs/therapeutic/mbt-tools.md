# MBT (Mentalization-Based Therapy) Tools for Ollie

## Overview

Mentalization is the ability to understand behavior — your own and others' — in terms of mental states (thoughts, feelings, desires, intentions). MBT was developed for BPD but helps anyone who struggles with emotional intensity, relationship difficulties, or black-and-white thinking.

---

## What Is Mentalization?

**Mentalizing** = understanding that:
- People have minds
- Minds contain thoughts, feelings, intentions
- These mental states drive behavior
- We can't read minds — we can only guess

**When we mentalize well**:
- Consider multiple perspectives
- Hold uncertainty ("I might be wrong")
- Understand our own reactions
- Respond rather than react

**When mentalization breaks down**:
- Black-and-white thinking
- Certainty about others' intentions
- Reactive, impulsive responses
- Intense, overwhelming emotions
- Difficulty understanding own feelings

---

## Why Mentalization Breaks Down

### Triggers for Loss of Mentalization

1. **High emotion**: When activated, nuance disappears
2. **Attachment threat**: Fear of abandonment, rejection
3. **Past trauma activation**: Old patterns take over
4. **Stress and exhaustion**: Depleted capacity
5. **Conflict**: Especially with close others

### What Happens

**Pre-mentalizing modes** take over:

- **Psychic equivalence**: "I feel it, so it's true"
- **Pretend mode**: Disconnected, intellectual, not feeling
- **Teleological stance**: Only actions count, not intentions

## Tools for Ollie

### Affect Labeling

**Purpose**: Name emotions to regulate them

**The practice**:
- Notice what you're feeling
- Put a specific word to it
- Differentiate (angry vs. hurt vs. scared)
- Notice where it lives in your body

**Why it helps**: Naming activates prefrontal cortex, reduces amygdala reactivity

**App Implementation**:
- Emotion vocabulary builder
- "What are you feeling? Let's get specific"
- Wheel of emotions reference
- Track emotional awareness over time

**Ollie prompt**:
> "You said 'bad.' Can we get more specific? Is it more like sad, angry, scared, hurt, or something else?"

### Slowing Down

**Purpose**: Create space for reflection before reaction

**The practice**:
- Notice the urge to react
- Pause before acting
- Ask: "What am I actually feeling?"
- Ask: "What might be driving this?"

**App Implementation**:
- Part of STOP skill (DBT crossover)
- "Before you respond..." prompts
- Breathing space before processing

### Perspective Taking

**Purpose**: Consider other viewpoints

**The practice**:
1. What do I think happened?
2. What might the other person have been thinking?
3. What might they have been feeling?
4. What might their intention have been?
5. Are there other explanations?

**App Implementation**:
- Guided reflection for interpersonal situations
- Multiple perspective prompts
- Hold uncertainty: "might have" language
- Not about being right, about being open

**Ollie prompts**:
> "What might they have been feeling in that moment?"
> "Is there another way to understand why they did that?"
> "What were you hoping they would do?"

### Understanding Your Own Mind

**Purpose**: Mentalize the self

**Questions to explore**:
- What am I actually feeling?
- What triggered this?
- What does this remind me of?
- What do I need right now?
- What am I afraid of?
- What do I want?

**App Implementation**:
- Part of check-in flow
- Reflection Mode deep dives
- Connect current reaction to past experiences

### The "Might" Exercise

**Purpose**: Build uncertainty tolerance

**The practice**:
Replace certainty statements with "might":
- "They hate me" → "They might be upset, or something else might be going on"
- "I always fail" → "I might struggle with this, or it might go differently"
- "They did it on purpose" → "They might have intended this, or not"

**App Implementation**:
- Thought reframing tool
- Builds flexibility
- Good for BPD black-and-white patterns

## Mentalization in Relationships

### Common Mentalization Failures

**In conflict**:
- Mind-reading: "You obviously don't care"
- Certainty: "I know why you did that"
- Extremes: "You always" / "You never"
- Intention-assuming: "You meant to hurt me"

**In self-reflection**:
- Confusion: "I don't know what I feel"
- Overwhelm: Feelings without words
- Certainty: "I'm definitely worthless"
- Disconnection: "I feel nothing"

### Repair Questions

**After conflict**:
- "What was I feeling during that?"
- "What might they have been feeling?"
- "Did I check my assumptions?"
- "What did I need that I didn't ask for?"

**App Implementation**:
- Post-conflict reflection tool
- Journaling prompts
- Pattern tracking over time

## Building Mentalization Capacity

### When Calm (Practice Mode)

- Reflect on past interactions
- Consider multiple perspectives
- Notice your own mental states
- Build emotional vocabulary

### When Activated (Recovery Mode)

- Don't try to figure it out now
- Ground first (breathe, 5-4-3-2-1)
- Wait for calm
- Then reflect

**Key principle**: You can't mentalize when dysregulated. Calm first, understand later.

---

## Integration with Ollie's Model

### Right Now Mode
- NOT the time for mentalizing
- Regulate first
- Simple affect labeling at most
- "What's the feeling?" not "Why?"

### Daily Mode
- Brief affect labeling in check-in
- Notice patterns of reactivity
- "Something activated me yesterday" tracking

### Reflection Mode (Primary Use)
- Perspective-taking exercises
- Relationship reflection
- Understanding triggers
- Building emotional vocabulary
- Pattern recognition

## MBT for BPD

### Specifically Helpful Because

1. **Reduces splitting**: Multiple perspectives reduce black/white
2. **Increases self-understanding**: Know what triggers you
3. **Improves relationships**: Understanding ≠ agreeing
4. **Reduces impulsivity**: Pause and reflect
5. **Builds identity**: Consistent self-understanding

### Key Interventions

- Affect labeling (reduces overwhelm)
- Uncertainty practice (reduces certainty-based reactions)
- Perspective taking (reduces personalization)
- Slowing down (reduces impulsivity)

## Ollie's MBT Voice

**Encouraging curiosity**:
> "I wonder what was going on for you in that moment?"

**Introducing uncertainty**:
> "What might they have been feeling? We can't know for sure, but we can wonder."

**Validating difficulty**:
> "When emotions are this strong, it's really hard to see other perspectives. That's completely normal."

**Building capacity**:
> "You're getting better at noticing what you feel before you react. That's mentalization in action."

**After reflection**:
> "You considered their perspective, even when you disagree. That takes strength."

**Normalizing limits**:
> "Nobody mentalizes perfectly all the time. Especially when we're hurt or scared."

---

## Red Flags: When to Ground First

- User is highly activated
- Certainty language: "definitely," "always," "never"
- Mind-reading statements
- Rapid, reactive responses
- Difficulty identifying any feelings

**Ollie's response**:
> "There's a lot happening right now. Let's slow down before we figure it out. Can we breathe together first?"

---

## Sample Reflection Prompts

**For self-understanding**:
> "When that happened, what did you feel in your body? What emotion might that be?"

**For relationship situations**:
> "You felt hurt when they said that. What do you think they might have been trying to communicate?"

**For pattern recognition**:
> "This seems to come up when you feel excluded. Does that connect to anything from earlier in your life?"

**For building flexibility**:
> "You're sure they don't like you. What if there were other explanations? Even if unlikely, what might they be?"

**For integration**:
> "What did you learn about yourself from reflecting on this?"

---

## Key Principles Summary

1. **Curiosity over certainty**: "I wonder" not "I know"
2. **Regulate before reflect**: Can't mentalize when activated
3. **Multiple perspectives**: Including your own
4. **Uncertainty is okay**: We can't read minds
5. **Slow down**: Pause before reacting
6. **Name to tame**: Label emotions specifically
7. **Behaviors have reasons**: Even if not obvious
