# DBT (Dialectical Behavior Therapy) Tools for Ollie

## Overview

DBT was developed by Marsha Linehan for people who experience emotions intensely. It combines acceptance strategies with change strategies - the "dialectic" of accepting yourself AND working on change.

---

## Core Philosophy

**The main dialectic**: "I am doing the best I can AND I need to do better."

This validates the struggle while encouraging growth. Never shame for current state.

---

## Four Skill Modules

### 1. Distress Tolerance
**Purpose**: Survive crisis moments without making things worse

### 2. Emotion Regulation  
**Purpose**: Understand and manage emotions effectively

### 3. Interpersonal Effectiveness
**Purpose**: Ask for what you need, say no, maintain relationships

### 4. Mindfulness
**Purpose**: Be present without judgment

---

## Distress Tolerance Skills

### TIPP (Change Body Chemistry Fast)

**T - Temperature**
- Hold ice cubes
- Splash cold water on face
- Cold shower

**I - Intense Exercise**
- Running, jumping jacks
- Anything that gets heart rate up
- Even for just 5-10 minutes

**P - Paced Breathing**
- Breathe out longer than in
- 4 seconds in, 6-8 seconds out
- Activates parasympathetic system

**P - Progressive Relaxation**
- Tense and release muscle groups
- Start from toes, work up
- Notice the contrast

**App Implementation**:
- Quick access from home screen
- Timer for paced breathing
- Simple guided progressive relaxation
- Track what works best for user

### STOP (Before Reacting)

**S - Stop**
- Don't react immediately
- Freeze in place

**T - Take a Step Back**
- Breathe
- Don't act on impulse

**O - Observe**
- What's happening inside and outside?
- What are the facts?

**P - Proceed Mindfully**
- Consider options
- Act with awareness

**App Implementation**:
- Crisis button leads here
- Simple, calm walkthrough
- Not too many words

### ACCEPTS (Distraction)

**A - Activities**: Do something else
**C - Contributing**: Help someone
**C - Comparisons**: Compare to harder times you survived
**E - Emotions**: Generate different emotions (funny video)
**P - Pushing Away**: Mentally set aside for now
**T - Thoughts**: Count, puzzle, focus mind elsewhere
**S - Sensations**: Strong sensations (ice, spicy, loud music)

**App Implementation**:
- Personalized list of user's activities
- Quick suggestions for each category
- No pressure to do all

### Radical Acceptance

**Concept**: Fighting reality causes more suffering. Acceptance ≠ approval.

**Formula**: Pain + Non-acceptance = Suffering

**When to use**: When you can't change the situation

**Key phrases**:
- "It is what it is"
- "This is the reality I'm facing"
- "I don't have to like it to accept it"
- "Fighting this won't change it"

**App Implementation**:
- Guided reflection
- Not for trauma without support
- Gentle, not forced

### Pros and Cons

**When considering impulsive behavior**:

|  | Acting on Urge | Not Acting |
|--|---------------|------------|
| Pros |  |  |
| Cons |  |  |

**Include**: Short-term and long-term for each

**App Implementation**:
- Four-quadrant worksheet
- Save for future reference
- Review when urge returns

## Emotion Regulation Skills

### PLEASE (Reduce Vulnerability)

**PL - Treat Physical Illness**
- See doctors
- Take medications as prescribed

**E - Balanced Eating**
- Regular meals
- Avoid too much/little

**A - Avoid Mood-Altering Substances**
- Alcohol, drugs affect emotion regulation

**S - Balanced Sleep**
- Regular schedule
- 7-9 hours

**E - Exercise**
- Regular movement
- Even walking counts

**App Implementation**:
- Gentle daily check: "How are your basics today?"
- Not tracking streaks
- Educational content about why these matter

### Check the Facts

**Before assuming emotions match reality**:
1. What event triggered the emotion?
2. What are my interpretations?
3. Am I assuming a threat?
4. What's the probability of threat?
5. What's the worst outcome?
6. Could I cope with it?
7. Does my emotion fit the facts?

**App Implementation**:
- Guided walkthrough
- Works well with thought records
- For when emotions feel very intense

### Opposite Action

**When emotion doesn't fit the facts or isn't effective**:

| Emotion | Urge | Opposite Action |
|---------|------|-----------------|
| Fear | Avoid | Approach |
| Anger | Attack | Gently avoid, be kind |
| Sadness | Withdraw | Get active, reach out |
| Shame | Hide | Share with safe person |
| Guilt (unjustified) | Apologize/fix | Don't apologize |

**Key**: Must do it fully, not half-heartedly

**App Implementation**:
- Identify emotion first
- Suggest opposite actions
- Track effectiveness

### Build Mastery

**Do something each day that builds competence**:
- Small, achievable tasks
- Gradually increase difficulty
- Sense of accomplishment

**App Implementation**:
- Integrates with habit/goal tracking
- Focus on effort, not outcome

### Cope Ahead

**Plan for difficult situations**:
1. Describe the situation coming up
2. What emotions might arise?
3. What skills could help?
4. Imagine using the skills successfully
5. Practice in mind

**App Implementation**:
- Before difficult events
- Save plans for reference

## Interpersonal Effectiveness

### DEAR MAN (Getting What You Need)

**D - Describe**: State facts without judgment
**E - Express**: Share feelings/opinions with "I" statements
**A - Assert**: Ask clearly for what you want
**R - Reinforce**: Explain positive outcomes of getting it

**M - Mindful**: Stay focused, don't get sidetracked
**A - Appear Confident**: Even if you don't feel it
**N - Negotiate**: Be willing to give to get

### GIVE (Maintaining Relationships)

**G - Gentle**: No attacks, threats, or judgments
**I - Interested**: Listen, don't interrupt
**V - Validate**: Acknowledge their perspective
**E - Easy Manner**: Smile, be light when appropriate

### FAST (Maintaining Self-Respect)

**F - Fair**: To yourself and others
**A - No Apologies**: Don't over-apologize
**S - Stick to Values**: Don't abandon what matters
**T - Truthful**: Don't lie or act helpless

**App Implementation**:
- These are more educational/reference
- Prep before difficult conversations
- Not for acute distress

## Mindfulness (DBT Style)

### What Skills

**Observe**: Notice without words
**Describe**: Put words to experience
**Participate**: Fully engage in the moment

### How Skills

**Non-judgmentally**: No good/bad labels
**One-mindfully**: One thing at a time
**Effectively**: Do what works

**App Implementation**:
- Short mindfulness exercises
- Emphasize non-judgment
- Practice observation and description

## DBT Diary Card

**Traditional diary card tracks daily**:
- Emotions (0-5)
- Urges (0-5)
- Actions (did behavior or not)
- Skills used

**Ollie Adaptation**:
- Simplified version
- Focus on skills used, not just problems
- Optional, not mandatory
- Review patterns over time

**Implementation**:
- Quick daily check-in option
- Multi-select skills used
- Mood/emotion intensity
- Notes (optional)

## When to Suggest DBT Tools

| Situation | Suggested Skills |
|-----------|-----------------|
| Intense emotion overwhelming | TIPP, STOP |
| Urge to do something harmful | STOP, Pros and Cons, Opposite Action |
| Can't stop thinking about something | ACCEPTS, Mindfulness |
| Feeling emotionally vulnerable | PLEASE |
| Before a hard conversation | DEAR MAN, GIVE, Cope Ahead |
| After a conflict | Check the Facts, Radical Acceptance |
| General distress | Grounding, Breathing |

---

## Tone for DBT in Ollie

**Validate first, always**:
- "It makes sense you're feeling this intensely"
- "Your emotions are real and valid"
- "This is really hard"

**Then offer skills**:
- "Would you like to try something that might help?"
- "There's a skill for moments like this"

**Never**:
- "Just calm down"
- "You're overreacting"
- "This isn't worth getting upset about"

---

## Sample Ollie Prompts

**Crisis moment**:
"I can see things are really intense right now. You don't have to figure anything out - let's just get through this moment. Would you like to try something to bring down the intensity?"

**After TIPP**:
"How are you feeling now compared to a few minutes ago? Even a small shift is something."

**Offering Radical Acceptance**:
"Sometimes when we can't change something, fighting against it adds to our pain. This doesn't mean liking what happened - just acknowledging that it is what it is. Would you like to explore this?"

**Daily check-in**:
"How are your basics today? Sleep, eating, movement - these small things affect how we handle everything else."
