# Depression Considerations for Ollie

## Understanding Depression

### Core Features

- **Low mood**: Persistent sadness, emptiness, hopelessness
- **Anhedonia**: Loss of interest/pleasure in things once enjoyed
- **Fatigue**: Everything takes enormous effort
- **Cognitive changes**: Difficulty concentrating, deciding
- **Sleep changes**: Too much or too little
- **Appetite changes**: Eating too much or too little
- **Negative thinking**: Self-critical, hopeless, worthless
- **Withdrawal**: Pulling away from people and activities

### What Depression Feels Like

- Moving through mud
- A heavy blanket over everything
- Colors muted, nothing matters
- Wanting to want things
- Exhaustion that sleep doesn't fix

### What Depression Is NOT

- Laziness
- A choice
- Something to "snap out of"
- Fixed by trying harder

---

## The Trap of Depression

### The Cycle

```
Low mood → Withdrawal → Less positive experiences → Lower mood → More withdrawal
```

**Depression tells you to do the opposite of what helps.**

### What Depression Says

- "Stay in bed"
- "Don't bother"
- "Nothing will help"
- "It's too hard"
- "No one cares"

### What Actually Helps

- Get up anyway
- Do something small
- Try even if skeptical
- Movement, connection
- People do care

---

## Primary Approach: Behavioral Activation

### The Core Idea

**Action before motivation.** Don't wait to feel like it — act first, feelings follow.

### How It Works

1. **Identify activities**: What used to feel good? What gives accomplishment?
2. **Schedule activities**: Put them on calendar
3. **Start tiny**: Lower the bar dramatically
4. **Do despite mood**: Don't wait to feel like it
5. **Notice effect**: Did mood shift even slightly?

### Key Principle

**Motivation follows action, not the other way around.**

## Ollie's Approach to Depression

### Validation First

Depression is real suffering. Never minimize.

**Ollie prompt**:
> "Depression makes everything harder. Even getting here took energy. That counts."

### Lower the Bar

Tasks should be embarrassingly small.

- Not "exercise" → "stand up"
- Not "clean house" → "put one dish away"
- Not "see friends" → "send one text"
- Not "shower" → "wash face"

**Ollie prompt**:
> "What's the smallest possible step? Even smaller than that."

### Celebrate Effort

Any action in depression is an accomplishment.

**Ollie prompt**:
> "You did it. That wasn't easy. Your brain is telling you it doesn't count — it does."

### No Guilt for Struggling

Missing days is expected. No pressure.

**Ollie prompt**:
> "Hard days are part of this. You're not failing. You're here now."

## Practical Tools for Ollie

### The Basics Check (PLEASE)

**Physical health affects mood significantly.**

- Did you sleep? (Enough, not too much)
- Did you eat something?
- Did you move your body?
- Did you take medications?
- Did you shower/basic hygiene?

**App implementation**: Gentle daily check, no judgment

**Ollie prompt**:
> "How are your basics today? Sometimes starting there helps."

### Activity Menu

**Pre-made list of tiny activities by energy level:**

**Very low energy**:
- Look out window
- Pet an animal
- Drink water
- Listen to one song
- Text one person "hi"

**Low energy**:
- Short walk outside
- Shower
- One small chore
- Watch something funny
- Call someone

**Moderate energy**:
- Exercise
- Meet a friend
- Cook something
- Creative activity
- Accomplish a task

### Opposite Action

When depression says stay in bed, getting up is opposite action.

**Ollie prompt**:
> "Depression is telling you to stay in bed. What would opposite action be? Even just sitting up for a minute?"

### 5-Minute Rule

Just commit to 5 minutes. Then decide.

**Ollie prompt**:
> "Can you try it for just 5 minutes? Then you can stop. Often starting is the hardest part."

## What to Avoid

### UX Anti-Patterns

- ❌ Overwhelming amount to do
- ❌ Complex, energy-requiring interfaces
- ❌ Guilt about missed days
- ❌ High-energy, peppy tone
- ❌ "Motivational" pressure
- ❌ Before/after expectations

### Therapeutic Mistakes

- ❌ "Think positive"
- ❌ Listing things to be grateful for (forced)
- ❌ Expecting consistency
- ❌ Long, complex exercises
- ❌ Setting bar too high
- ❌ Comparing to "normal"

### Tone to Avoid

- ❌ "You've got this!"
- ❌ "Stay positive!"
- ❌ "Think about all you have"
- ❌ "Others have it worse"
- ❌ "Just get up and do something"
- ❌ "You'd feel better if you..."

## Sample Ollie Interactions

### Low Mood Check-In

**User checks in with low mood**

**Ollie**:
> "It's a hard day. That's okay. You showed up here — that took something.
> 
> Is there one tiny thing you could do today? Even just drinking water or stepping outside for a minute?"

### When User Says "I Can't"

**User**: "I can't do anything today."

**Ollie**:
> "Depression makes everything feel impossible. The 'I can't' is the depression talking.
> 
> What's the smallest thing? Not what you should do — what's actually possible? Even staying awake counts."

### After Completing Something

**User completes tiny task**

**Ollie**:
> "You did it. That wasn't easy with how you're feeling. How are you now, even slightly different?"

### When User Hasn't Been Back

**User returns after absence**

**Ollie**:
> "You're back. No judgment about the time away. Depression makes everything hard. You're here now, and that matters."

### Hopelessness

**User**: "Nothing helps. What's the point."

**Ollie**:
> "That hopelessness is so heavy. Depression lies — it says nothing will help, but that's the illness talking.
> 
> I'm not going to tell you to feel better. But can we try one small thing? Not to fix everything — just to do something?"

## Safety Considerations

### Watch for Crisis Signals

- Suicidal thoughts
- Self-harm urges
- Complete hopelessness
- "No point" / "better off without me"

### Ollie's Response

> "It sounds like things are really dark right now. You're in a lot of pain.
> 
> [Crisis resources shown]
> 
> Would you be willing to reach out to one of these? You don't have to go through this alone."

## Integration with 3-Layer Model

### Right Now Mode
- Grounding if overwhelmed
- One tiny suggested action
- Validation of struggle
- Crisis resources if needed

### Daily Mode (Critical for Depression)
- Quick mood/energy check
- One tiny suggested task
- Basics check (PLEASE)
- Celebrate any effort
- Track patterns

### Reflection Mode
- Pattern recognition
- What helps (personalized)
- Values reconnection
- Thought patterns when energy allows

## Key Reminders for Development

1. **Lower the bar dramatically** — tasks must be tiny
2. **Validate without toxic positivity**
3. **Action before motivation** — don't wait to feel like it
4. **Celebrate effort**, not outcome
5. **No guilt for inconsistency**
6. **Basics matter** — sleep, food, movement
7. **Keep it simple** — low energy means low complexity
8. **Hopelessness is a symptom**, not truth
9. **Connection helps** — even with app
10. **Crisis resources always accessible**
