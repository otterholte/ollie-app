# Anxiety Considerations for Ollie

## Understanding Anxiety

### What Anxiety Is

- A normal human emotion (protective)
- Becomes a problem when excessive, persistent, or interfering
- Involves body, thoughts, and behaviors
- Fight/flight/freeze response when no real danger

### Core Features

- **Excessive worry**: About many things, hard to control
- **Physical symptoms**: Racing heart, tension, breathing changes, stomach issues
- **Avoidance**: Escaping or avoiding feared situations
- **Catastrophizing**: Expecting worst outcomes
- **Need for certainty**: Difficulty with "maybe"

### Anxiety Types

- Generalized Anxiety (GAD): Worry about everything
- Social Anxiety: Fear of judgment
- Panic Disorder: Unexpected panic attacks
- Specific Phobias: Particular fears
- Health Anxiety: Fear of illness

## What Works for Anxiety

### 1. Regulate the Body First

When anxious, the body is activated. Cognitive strategies don't work well until the body calms.

**Tools**:
- Paced breathing (longer exhale)
- Grounding (5-4-3-2-1)
- Cold water/temperature change
- Progressive muscle relaxation

### 2. Accept, Don't Fight

Fighting anxiety makes it worse. Acceptance reduces suffering.

**ACT approach**:
- Make room for anxious feelings
- Anxiety isn't dangerous
- You can have anxiety AND live your life

### 3. Face Fears Gradually

Avoidance maintains anxiety. Gradual exposure reduces it.

**Principles**:
- Start small
- Stay until anxiety naturally decreases
- Repeat until easier
- Build tolerance, not elimination

### 4. Work with Thoughts (When Calm)

CBT thought work helps when not in acute anxiety.

**Tools**:
- Check the facts
- Probability estimates
- What would I tell a friend?
- Best/worst/most likely scenarios

## Tools by Therapy

### DBT
- **TIPP**: Body-based regulation
- **Distress tolerance**: Get through without making worse
- **Check the Facts**: Is threat real?
- **Opposite Action**: Approach instead of avoid

### ACT
- **Acceptance**: Make room for anxiety
- **Defusion**: "I notice I'm feeling anxious"
- **Values**: What matters more than the fear?
- **Committed Action**: Do it afraid

### CBT
- **Thought records**: Examine anxious predictions
- **Behavioral experiments**: Test predictions
- **Exposure hierarchies**: Gradual facing
- **Decatastrophizing**: Best/worst/likely

### MBCT/Mindfulness
- **Grounding**: Anchor to present
- **3-minute breathing space**: Reset
- **Name and notice**: Label the anxiety
- **Body awareness**: Where do you feel it?

## Ollie's Approach to Anxiety

### In Right Now Mode

**Goal**: Calm the body, get through the moment

1. Validate: "Anxiety is really uncomfortable. Let's work with it."
2. Regulate: Breathing, grounding, TIPP
3. Accept: "This feeling will pass. It's not dangerous."
4. Don't analyze or problem-solve yet

**Ollie prompt**:
> "Your body is activated right now. Let's help it calm down before we think through anything. Can you try some slow breaths with me?"

### In Daily Mode

- Check anxiety levels
- One small brave action (optional)
- Pattern tracking
- Worry time scheduling

### In Reflection Mode

- What triggers anxiety?
- What helps?
- Thought patterns to examine
- Values despite fear

## Specific Techniques

### Paced Breathing

**Why**: Longer exhale activates parasympathetic system

**How**:
- Inhale 4 seconds
- Exhale 6-8 seconds
- Repeat 5-10 times

**App implementation**: Visual guide, timer

### 5-4-3-2-1 Grounding

**Why**: Anchors to present moment

**How**:
- 5 things you see
- 4 things you hear
- 3 things you feel (touch)
- 2 things you smell
- 1 thing you taste

### Worry Time

**Why**: Contains worry to specific time

**How**:
- Schedule 15-30 min daily
- Write down worries during day
- During worry time, address them
- Outside worry time, postpone

**App implementation**: Worry capture and scheduled review

### Best/Worst/Most Likely

**Why**: Balanced thinking about outcomes

**How**:
- What's the worst that could happen?
- What's the best that could happen?
- What's most likely?
- Can I cope with likely?

### Opposite Action

**Why**: Approach breaks avoidance cycle

**How**:
- Notice urge to avoid
- Do the opposite (approach)
- Act confident even if don't feel it
- Stay until anxiety decreases

## What to Avoid

### UX Anti-Patterns

- ❌ Panic-inducing design (fast, red, urgent)
- ❌ Pressure to complete things
- ❌ Complex, overwhelming interfaces
- ❌ Time pressure
- ❌ Notifications that spike anxiety

### Therapeutic Mistakes

- ❌ Excessive reassurance
- ❌ Problem-solving while activated
- ❌ Challenging thoughts during panic
- ❌ Pushing exposure too fast
- ❌ "Just relax" / "Don't worry"

### Tone to Avoid

- ❌ "There's nothing to worry about"
- ❌ "Everything will be fine"
- ❌ "Just calm down"
- ❌ "It's all in your head"
- ❌ "You're overthinking"

## Panic Attack Support

### What Happens in Panic

- Sudden surge of fear
- Physical symptoms (heart racing, can't breathe, dizzy)
- Feels like dying, going crazy, losing control
- Peaks in 10 minutes, passes

### Ollie's Response

1. Validate: "This is scary. It's not dangerous."
2. Ground: Feet on floor, cold water
3. Breathe: Slow exhale
4. Remind: "This will pass. It always does."
5. Stay with: "I'm here."

**Ollie prompt**:
> "I know this feels overwhelming. Your body is having a big reaction, but you're safe. This will pass. Can you feel your feet on the floor?"

## Sample Ollie Interactions

### Acute Anxiety

**User**: "I can't stop worrying about tomorrow. My chest is tight."

**Ollie**:
> "Your body is telling you something feels threatening. Let's help it settle first.
> 
> Can you take a slow breath? Exhale longer than you inhale. I'll guide you."
> 
> [Breathing guide]
> 
> "When you're ready, we can look at what's worrying you. But body first."

### Avoidance

**User**: "I want to skip the party. I'll just embarrass myself."

**Ollie**:
> "Social situations bring up a lot. Avoiding feels safer, but it keeps the anxiety strong.
> 
> What if you went for just 30 minutes? You can leave anytime. What matters to you about being there?"

### Worry Spiral

**User**: "I keep thinking about everything that could go wrong."

**Ollie**:
> "Your mind is really active with 'what ifs.' That's exhausting.
> 
> Would you like to write these worries down and save them for a worry time later? That way they're not lost, but they don't have to run the show right now."

## Key Reminders for Development

1. Calm the body before engaging the mind
2. Design should be calming (slow, soft, spacious)
3. Acceptance before change
4. Exposure should be gradual and supported
5. Worry time contains rumination
6. Validate without excessive reassurance
7. Panic attacks pass — emphasize this
8. Avoidance is the enemy, approach is the friend
9. Keep tools quick and accessible
10. No pressure, no urgency in the app
