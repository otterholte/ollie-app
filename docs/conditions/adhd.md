# ADHD Considerations for Ollie

## Understanding ADHD

### Core Challenges

- **Executive dysfunction**: Difficulty initiating, planning, organizing
- **Time blindness**: Poor sense of time passing
- **Emotional dysregulation**: Intense emotions, quick to frustrate
- **Rejection sensitivity**: Deep pain from perceived rejection
- **Inconsistent motivation**: Interest-based, not importance-based
- **Working memory issues**: Forgetting mid-task
- **Hyperfocus AND distractibility**: Extremes

### What ADHD Is NOT

- Laziness
- Not caring
- A motivation problem
- Something you can "just try harder" to fix

---

## What Works for ADHD Users

### App Design Principles

1. **Keep it SHORT**
   - No long text walls
   - One thing at a time
   - Skip option always available

2. **Make it FAST**
   - Quick check-ins (under 30 seconds possible)
   - Immediate results
   - No multi-step processes required

3. **Add NOVELTY**
   - Rotate suggestions
   - Variety in tools
   - Fresh content over time

4. **External STRUCTURE**
   - The app decides, not the user
   - Clear prompts, not open questions
   - Reduce decision fatigue

5. **No GUILT**
   - Missed days expected
   - No streaks
   - "You're back" not "You missed 5 days"

## Helpful Tools from Each Therapy

### From CBT
- **Quick thought capture**: One field, done
- **Behavioral experiments**: Small, concrete
- NOT: Long thought records, complex worksheets

### From DBT
- **TIPP**: Physical, immediate
- **Distress tolerance**: Action-based
- **STOP**: Brief, memorable
- Diary cards: SIMPLIFIED version only

### From ACT
- **Values clarity**: What matters TODAY
- **Defusion**: Quick phrases
- **Committed action**: ONE tiny step

### From Mindfulness
- **Very short practices**: 1-3 minutes max
- **Body-based**: Less thinking
- **Grounding**: Sensory anchors
- NOT: Long meditations

## ADHD-Specific Features

### Body Doubling Timer
- Timer for tasks
- "Ollie is here with you"
- Visual countdown
- Gentle completion celebration

### Dopamine Menu
- Pre-made list of quick dopamine hits
- User adds their own
- Suggests when motivation is low
- Healthy options (not just screens)

### Habit Stacking
- Connect new habits to existing ones
- "After [existing habit], I will [new habit]"
- Leverage autopilot

### Task Breakdown
- Take any task
- Break into tiny steps
- First step is ridiculously small
- "What's the FIRST physical action?"

### Time Anchors
- Connect check-ins to existing routines
- Morning coffee, lunch, bedtime
- Not arbitrary times

## What to Avoid

### UX Anti-Patterns for ADHD

- ❌ Long onboarding flows
- ❌ Required reading before using
- ❌ Multi-step processes to start
- ❌ Open-ended questions ("How are you feeling?")
- ❌ Text-heavy screens
- ❌ Streak pressure
- ❌ Complex navigation
- ❌ Features that require consistency
- ❌ Guilt for inconsistency

### Tone to Avoid

- ❌ "You should..."
- ❌ "Make sure you..."
- ❌ "Don't forget to..."
- ❌ "You missed..."
- ❌ "Try harder"
- ❌ "Just do it"

## Rejection Sensitivity (RSD)

### What It Is
- Extreme emotional pain from perceived rejection
- Can be triggered by criticism, disappointment, failure
- Physical-level pain response
- Often misunderstood as "overreacting"

### How Ollie Handles It
- Validate the intensity ("That really hurt")
- Don't minimize ("They probably didn't mean it")
- Offer grounding before processing
- Self-compassion tools
- NOT immediate cognitive restructuring

---

## Emotional Dysregulation

### How It Shows Up
- Intense emotional reactions
- Difficulty calming down
- Frustration spirals
- Mood shifts

### What Helps
- Regulation first (TIPP, breathing)
- Physical outlet options
- Validation of intensity
- Wait before processing

**Ollie's approach**:
> "Your feelings are big right now. That's okay. Let's help your body calm first."

## Sample Ollie Interactions

### Quick Check-In
> "How's your energy?" [Low/Mid/High buttons]
> "One word for right now?" [Quick input]
> Done. That's the check-in.

### Suggesting a Tool
> "Need a reset? Try this 60-second breathing."
> [Single button to start]

### When User Returns After Absence
> "You're here. That's what matters."
> (NOT: "It's been 5 days!")

### Breaking Down a Task
> "What's one thing you want to do?"
> "What's the very first physical step? Like, picking up a pen, opening an app..."
> "Just do that one thing. The rest can wait."

### Motivation Support
> "Your brain needs interest to move. What's one tiny way to make this more interesting?"
> "Or: what can you do for just 5 minutes?"

## Integration with 3-Layer Model

### Right Now Mode
- TIPP for emotional intensity
- Very quick grounding
- Physical options (movement, temperature)
- No thinking required

### Daily Mode
- 10-second check-in option
- One tiny suggested task
- Body doubling timer available
- Dopamine menu for motivation

### Reflection Mode
- Short sessions
- Pattern recognition (what works)
- Optional, never required
- Exit anytime

---

## Key Reminders for Development

1. Every feature should be usable in under 2 minutes
2. Always have a "skip" or "not now" option
3. Reduce choices (Ollie decides)
4. Celebrate showing up, not completing
5. Variety in suggestions
6. No punishment for inconsistency
7. Physical/action-based tools prioritized
8. Test with ADHD users early and often
